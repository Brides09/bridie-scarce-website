# Website Context

## Purpose of the website

This website exists to hold a body of reflective writing about the relationship between creativity, education, design, and artificial intelligence. It is not only a portfolio, and it is not only a blog. It is a publication that gathers essays, profiles, field notes, and thinking around the question of how people create and learn in a time of rapid technological change.

The site should feel like a journal and a magazine at once: personal, thoughtful, and connected.

## Long-term vision

The long-term vision is to build a living publication that grows over time without losing its core identity. The site should continue to feel:

- reflective rather than performative
- intellectually serious without becoming academic or dry
- connected across articles and themes
- rooted in lived experience and human judgement
- open to new ideas, new people, and new conversations

## Recurring themes

The publication repeatedly returns to a small set of ideas:

- attention and presence
- curiosity and experimentation
- creativity as a process, not just a result
- human judgement and discernment
- learning, making, and reflection
- systems thinking and thoughtful design
- the role of technology in supporting, not replacing, human thinking

These themes should continue to appear across the site, even when individual pieces focus on different people, places, or topics.

## Important people and places

The current body of work draws together voices and settings that help shape the publication’s wider conversation:

- Bobby Hill — studio practice, attention, process, and the value of slowing down
- Dr Tim Kitchen — education, creativity, and generative AI in learning environments
- Natalie Nixon — imagination, flourishing, and creative thinking
- Nabeel Barqawi — systems thinking and human-centred AI
- Xavier Rumph — design strategy and connected ways of working
- Matt Miller — workflows, systems, and the value of well-designed infrastructure
- Alon Grinshpoon — practical experimentation and the role of imagination in building
- Jack Axelrod — judgement, data, and the human question beneath technological progress
- Daisy Auger-Domínguez — leadership, creativity, and the future of work
- Dag Folger — structure, critical thinking, and the limits of automation
- Devon Turnbull — restoration, craft, and the value of making things that last

The site also carries the tension between two places that matter most to the publication’s voice: Melbourne and New York.

## Relationships between articles

The articles should not feel isolated. They are part of one larger conversation.

The main essay functions as the central thread. Other pieces deepen or reframe that thread:

- The field guide turns the ideas into practical habits and tools.
- Profile pieces give the themes a human face.
- The home page acts as a map of the publication’s recurring concerns.

Future content should strengthen this web of connection rather than creating a separate silo.

## Ongoing projects and directions

The site currently points toward a few ongoing editorial directions:

- a continuing series of essays about creativity and AI
- profiles of people whose work helps illuminate the publication’s themes
- a field guide for practical methods and habits worth keeping
- a reflective, magazine-style home experience that links ideas across the site

These directions should remain central even as the publication grows.

## Editorial guiding principle

The site should keep becoming more coherent over time. Each new piece should feel like it belongs to the same conversation, even when it approaches the topic from a different angle.
