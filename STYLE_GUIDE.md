# Style Guide

## Editorial tone

This publication should feel reflective, intelligent, and quietly confident. The tone is not academic in a dry sense, but it is thoughtful. It is also not promotional. It should feel like a magazine essay, a field note, and a conversation all at once.

Preferred qualities:

- calm and observant
- curious rather than assertive
- humane and considered
- precise without sounding clinical
- warm without becoming casual

## Writing conventions

- Use clear, elegant prose.
- Prefer plain language over ornamental phrasing.
- Keep sentences varied and readable.
- Avoid over-explaining ideas that are already clear.
- Do not overuse slogans or summary lines.
- Keep the writing grounded in lived experience when possible.

## Headings and structure

- Use short, confident headings that feel editorial rather than promotional.
- Let section headings support the mood of the piece.
- Use subheadings sparingly and only when they genuinely help the reader.
- Preserve a sense of flow between sections.

## Typography

The site already uses a defined visual system. Future edits should respect it rather than introducing new visual habits.

- Use the existing serif pairing for long-form reading and article titles.
- Use the mono style sparingly for labels, metadata, and small cues.
- Maintain strong readability and generous spacing.
- Do not turn the site into a dense, overloaded layout.

## Paragraphing and rhythm

- Keep paragraphs readable and varied in length.
- Allow pauses and emphasis through short sentences where appropriate.
- Let a paragraph end on a thought rather than forcing a conclusion.
- Avoid over-formatting the prose with too many callouts or device-like structures.

## Image captions

- Keep captions concise, descriptive, and understated.
- Avoid hype or sales language.
- Let captions support the mood of the image rather than dominate it.
- If an image is primarily decorative, avoid over-explaining it.

## Quotations and pull quotes

- Use quotations sparingly and intentionally.
- Prefer short, resonant lines over long block quotations.
- Pull quotes should feel like a moment of emphasis, not a slogan.
- Keep quotations faithful to the speaker and the tone of the piece.

## Internal linking

Internal links should help the reader move through the publication as a connected body of work.

- Link between related articles where the connection is genuine.
- Use subtle cross-references rather than obvious promotional links.
- Let links deepen the reader’s sense of continuity across the site.
- When linking to a related article, use the existing route conventions.

## Overall writing principles

- Preserve the writer’s authentic voice.
- Do not force everything into the same shape.
- Let essays, profiles, and notes feel distinct while still belonging to one publication.
- Maintain a contemplative, magazine-style tone.
- Keep the work intelligent, generous, and human.
