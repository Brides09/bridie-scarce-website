# CLAUDE.md

## Permanent instructions for future AI editing sessions

This project is a magazine-style publication about creativity, education, design, and artificial intelligence. The goal is not to turn it into a generic blog or a corporate marketing site. It should remain thoughtful, reflective, human, and quietly intellectual.

## My writing style

- Write with a calm, reflective, essayistic voice.
- Prefer measured observation over dramatic declaration.
- Keep the prose intimate, intelligent, and grounded in lived experience.
- Sound like a thoughtful writer, not a marketing copywriter.
- Let the ideas breathe rather than forcing them into a polished performance.

## Preferred editing approach

- Make small, careful changes.
- Do not rewrite unnecessarily.
- Preserve the authentic voice already present in the writing.
- Strengthen links between ideas, but do so subtly.
- Improve transitions when they help the reader move from one thought to the next.
- Prefer editorial refinement over structural overhauls.

## Audience

The audience is thoughtful readers interested in:

- creativity and making
- education and pedagogy
- design thinking
- technology and its human consequences
- reflective writing that treats ideas seriously but not stiffly

## HTML conventions

- This site is a static HTML/CSS/JavaScript project.
- Keep content in [index.html](index.html) unless there is a clear reason to add new files.
- Preserve the existing template structure.
- Reuse existing classes and semantic layout patterns where possible.
- Avoid introducing frameworks, build tools, or unnecessary JavaScript complexity.
- Keep the markup simple, readable, and accessible.

## Accessibility expectations

- Use semantic HTML structure.
- Maintain clear heading hierarchy.
- Keep links meaningful and descriptive.
- Use button text and labels that make sense out of context.
- Preserve keyboard accessibility and avoid introducing inaccessible interactions.
- Ensure contrast remains strong enough for reading.
- Decorative images should not create unnecessary noise for assistive technology.
- Do not make the site feel visually slick at the cost of clarity or usability.

## Respect for speakers’ intellectual property

- Respect the ideas, work, and public presence of the people featured in the site.
- Do not reproduce large excerpts, verbatim passages, or copyrighted material without permission.
- When referencing people or organisations, link where appropriate and keep the treatment respectful and accurate.
- Avoid turning the site into a promotional platform for individuals or institutions.
- Treat interviews, talks, and public work as material to be interpreted and discussed thoughtfully, not repackaged carelessly.

## Editorial and tonal principles

- Preserve the reflective magazine-style tone.
- Connect ideas across articles where it feels natural.
- Make the publication feel like one evolving conversation rather than separate pieces.
- Keep the writing grounded in curiosity, attention, process, and human judgement.
- Avoid over-claiming, over-selling, or over-explaining.
- Let the work feel considered, not over-polished.

## Editing checklist before finishing

Before finalising any edit, ask:

- Did I preserve the writer’s voice?
- Did I avoid unnecessary rewriting?
- Did I improve the experience for the reader without over-editing?
- Did I maintain accessibility and clarity?
- Did I help connect ideas across the publication?
- Does this still feel like Scarce Journal?
